# Setting Up RJ's Tree Care on Google (Free) — A Walkthrough for Ron

This is the single highest-impact free thing RJ's Tree Care can do online. When someone in Dillsboro, Rushville, Batesville, or anywhere nearby searches "tree service near me" on Google or Google Maps, this is what shows up — the map, the star rating, the phone number, the photos. It costs nothing and takes about 20 minutes to set up.

---

## Step 1: Claim or Create the Listing

1. Go to **google.com/business** (or just search "Google Business Profile" on Google).
2. Sign in with a Google account — if RJ's Tree Care doesn't have one yet, create a free one (e.g., `rjstreecare@gmail.com`). Use an email Ron checks regularly, since Google sends review notifications and customer messages here.
3. Search for "RJ's Tree Care" — if a listing already exists (sometimes Google creates one automatically from old directory data), claim it. If not, click "Add your business."

## Step 2: Enter the Real Business Info

Use exactly this — consistency matters for how Google ranks the listing:

- **Business name:** RJ's Tree Care
- **Category:** Tree service (primary). You can add secondary categories like "Landscaper" or "Stump removal service" if Google offers them.
- **Address:** 9588 Akes Hill Rd, Dillsboro, IN 47018
- **Service area:** Add it as a service-area business that also serves customers at your location. List the towns you actually run jobs in: Dillsboro, Rushville, Batesville, Moores Hill, Rising Sun, Aurora, Lawrenceburg, Greendale, plus the Cincinnati OH tri-state area, and Northern Kentucky towns toward Louisville (Florence, Bellevue, Newport, Covington, Alexandria, Highland Heights, Hebron, Burlington, Ludlow).
- **Phone:** (765) 570-3398
- **Website:** the RJ's Tree Care website URL (once it's live)
- **Hours:** whatever Ron's actual hours are — and make sure to mark whether 24/7 emergency calls are taken, since that's a real differentiator

## Step 3: Verify the Business

Google will need to confirm the business is real and at that address. Usually this is done one of two ways:

- **Postcard by mail** — Google mails a postcard with a verification code to the business address (takes about 5 days). You enter the code online once it arrives.
- **Phone or video verification** — sometimes offered instead, especially for service-area businesses without a public storefront.

Just follow whichever option Google offers — there's no way to skip this step, and an unverified listing won't show up in search results.

## Step 4: Add Real Photos

This matters more than people think — listings with photos get dramatically more calls and direction requests than listings without. Use real photos from real jobs:

- The truck and equipment
- Ron and the crew on a job (climbing, in the bucket, operating the stump grinder)
- Before/after shots of actual completed jobs (only ones that are genuinely documented — never use stock photos here)
- A profile photo / logo for the business

Upload at least 5–10 to start, and add new ones every few weeks as new jobs get done. Recent, real photos are one of the strongest trust signals on the whole listing.

## Step 5: Write the Business Description

Keep it short, honest, and in Ron's voice. Something like:

> "RJ's Tree Care is a family-owned tree service based in Dillsboro and Rushville, Indiana, with over 22 years of hands-on experience. We handle tree removal, trimming and pruning, stump grinding, and 24/7 emergency response — serving Southeast Indiana, the Cincinnati tri-state area, and Northern Kentucky toward Louisville. Licensed, insured, and known for one thing above all: we clean up like we wasn't even there."

## Step 6: Turn On Messaging and Q&A

Google lets customers message the business directly from the listing and ask public questions. Turn this on if Ron's comfortable responding to texts/messages — a fast reply to a Google message often turns directly into a booked job. If it gets to be too much, it can always be turned back off.

## The Most Important Part: Reviews

Right now RJ's Tree Care has a real 5-star rating — but only from **one review**. That's actually a big opportunity, not a weakness, because a handful of new genuine reviews will move the needle fast.

**How to get them — the honest way:**

1. **Ask at the right moment.** Right after a job wraps up and the customer is standing there looking at a clean yard and a tree that's gone (or trimmed up nicely) is the best moment to ask — people are happy and the work is fresh in their mind.
2. **Make it easy.** Every Google Business Profile has a direct review link. Once the listing is verified, Google (or this guide, once updated) can generate a short link like `g.page/r/XXXXXXX/review` that goes straight to the review box — no searching required. Text or hand that link to happy customers.
3. **Just ask in person or by text:** something simple like *"Hey, really appreciate the business — if you've got 30 seconds, a quick Google review would mean a lot and helps other folks in the area find us."* That's it. No incentives, no pressure, no fake reviews — just a genuine ask after a good job.
4. **Respond to every review**, good or bad. A short "Thanks so much, glad we could help!" shows future customers that someone's actually paying attention.

**Do NOT:**
- Buy reviews or use review-exchange services (Google actively detects and penalizes this — it can get a listing suspended)
- Offer discounts in exchange for reviews (against Google's policy and can backfire)
- Ask employees or family members to post reviews as if they were customers

A slow trickle of 1–2 real reviews a week from real customers will build a strong, trustworthy rating over a few months — and that's worth far more than a fast pile of fake ones.

## Why This Matters More Than Almost Anything Else

For a local service business like RJ's Tree Care, the Google Business Profile is usually the #1 source of new customer calls — often ahead of the website itself. People don't search "RJ's Tree Care" by name; they search "tree removal Dillsboro" or "emergency tree service near me," and Google Maps results are what shows up first. A complete, verified, photo-filled, review-backed profile is the single best lever to pull for getting the phone to ring — and it's completely free.

**Bottom line for Ron:** spend the 20 minutes to set it up, snap a few photos on the next couple jobs, and start asking happy customers for a quick review. That's it — everything compounds from there.
